# v10.0 Research: Cheap Logic Analyzer to Probe the D330 Panel

**Question:** what instrument to buy, and how to physically probe the D330's panel flex, to capture the GOP's panel-init wire traffic at cold boot vs the kernel's re-probe at S3 resume?

**Verdict (one line):** buy the **Kingst LA5016 ($238)** — the only cheap analyzer with a built-in **MIPI-DSI LP-mode decoder** — optionally the **DSLogic U3Pro16 ($299)** for long stream captures; **nothing buyable at this price decodes DSI HS**, and that loss is acceptable because the panel-init DCS traffic we care about (VBT block 53) is flagged **LP mode**, and PMIC **I2C bus 6** carries the rail sequencing.

## 0. What the instrument is actually being asked to answer

From the v9 audit + GOP_DSI_RE_FINDINGS:

- Block 53 (MIPI sequence, decoded) says the panel sequences are: POWER_ON (GPIO 144), INIT_OTP (GPIO 160 toggle ×2), DISPLAY_ON = **LP-mode DCS** `0x11` → 200 ms → `0x29`, BACKLIGHT_ON (GPIO 145). There is **no panel register-init table** in block 53.
- Yet GOP reaches operational DSI state X=Y and does "direct TCON programming the kernel cannot see" (DPHY 0x162000+ reads all-ones). The kernel re-probe replays the block-53-equivalent path and still lands black (X=Z).
- So the wire-level open questions are exactly three:
  1. Does GOP send anything **on the DSI lanes** beyond the block-53 LP DCS bursts (e.g., HS-format DCS/MCS writes = the unseen TCON programming)?
  2. Does GOP toggle the **PMIC rails** (GPIO 144/160/145, over **PMIC I2C bus 6**) differently from what the kernel's resume path does?
  3. Does the kernel's re-probe send **any** DSI traffic / rail toggles at all, and how does it differ from boot?

That is an LP-mode + I2C + envelope-timing question, not a 1 Gbps decode question. Which is why a cheap LA is viable at all.

### 1.1 The sample-rate honesty table

What each signal actually needs (MIPI D-PHY: HS = differential, tens of MHz to >1 GHz clock, up to 4.5 Gbps/lane per D-PHY 2.0; LP/escape = clocking embedded in data, used for config/commands; source: Wikipedia DSI citing mipi.org):

| Signal on the panel flex | Rate | Minimum sample rate (≥5×) | Cheap LA verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| DSI **HS video/DCS** (this panel: 4-lane RGB888 non-burst, 1200×1920@60 portrait ≈ **1.0 Gbps/lane** computed from VBT mode) | ~1 Gbps/lane | 3–5 GS/s | **NO cheap instrument decodes this.** Saleae Pro 16 (500 MS/s) fails by 10×; even U3Pro16's 1 GHz mode (≤8ch) is 1 sample/bit = no decode. |
| DSI **LP escape bursts** (init DCS `0x11`/`0x29` — block 53 flags **LP**) | ~20 Mbps nominal (escape mode 10–100 Mbps class) | 100–200 MS/s | **YES** — LA5016 @500 MS/s = 25× oversample; LA2016 @200 = 10× |
| **PMIC I2C bus 6** (panel rails) | 100–400 kHz | 4–16 MS/s | **YES** — even the cheapest tools |
| Backlight PWM / EN / RST rails | kHz–MHz | any | YES |
| HS **presence/timing** (not content) via envelope | — | 100–200 MS/s with ~0.6 V threshold | **YES as a trick** — see §4.2 |

**Honest conclusion:** DSI HS content decode is a $5k+ pro-scope capability and is NOT part of this milestone. The realistic instrument job is LP DSI + I2C + rails + HS envelope timing. Documented as such.

## 2. Decoder landscape (what software can turn bytes into meaning)

- **KingstVIS (vendor, Windows/Mac/Linux) has a "MIPI-DSI LP" decoder — but only on LA5016/LA5032.** This is the single strongest reason to buy the LA5016: the D330's init DCS is LP-flagged, so the out-of-box decoder likely decodes the exact traffic we want. KingstVIS runs in a fully-featured demo mode without a device, so the decoder's usefulness can be validated *before* buying. (`http://www.qdkingst.com/en/plist`)
- **sigrok/libsigrokdecode: 131 decoders, NO MIPI DSI** (verified by scanning the entire official decoder list; its "DSI" entry is the *Digital Serial Interface lighting protocol*). DSView (DreamSourceLab's sigrok fork) inherits the same list — its published "DSI" decoder is also the lighting one.
- **Fallback if LA2016/U3Pro16 is bought instead:** a custom libsigrokdecode/DSView PD for LP-escape DSI (~200 lines of Python: detect LP-00 escape entry, SoT 0xB8, decode packet DI/WC/ECC/payload) — or manual decode in PulseView/DSView; LP bursts during init are short and few.
- **I2C decoding:** everywhere — KingstVIS, DSView, PulseView, scopes.
- **Saleae Logic 2:** no DSI analyzer; its HLA SDK could host one but no known ready-made MIPI DSI HLA was found. Another reason Saleae is not the pick.

## 3. Physical access (D330-10IGL, 82H0)

Confidence LOW-MEDIUM: no official schematic exists (Lenovo publishes only the free HMM — FRU locations and teardown steps, no pinouts). Leaked boardviews for 82H0 exist on enthusiast forums but are unverifiable from here and unstable — treat the board as **schematically unknown** and plan empirically.

- **Teardown:** tablet form; plastic back, pry from the frame; disconnect the battery before any probing (Lenovo HMM for D330 documents the steps: `https://support.lenovo.com` → D330 series → Hardware Maintenance Manual).
- **The probe target is the single wide panel flex** (mainboard → display assembly, portrait panel, 4-lane DSI + power + backlight). Expected connector class: ~40-pin, 0.5 mm-pitch FPC (**unverified — count pins and measure pitch at teardown before ordering anything**).
- **Pin identification without a schematic (empirical, in this order):**
  1. GND pins — continuity beeper to chassis shield.
  2. Power rails — powered-up DC voltages (3V3 panel VDD, backlight rail).
  3. DSI lanes — **both Dp and Dn idle at ~1.2 V (LP-11)**; that signature separates DSI pairs from PWM/EN. CLK pair behaves the same in video idle.
  4. Remaining single-ended lines = RST / backlight PWM / EN candidates — confirm by behavior at boot.
- **PMIC I2C bus 6:** the rails are PMIC GPIOs (VBT A: "PPS GPIO Pins: Using PMIC, MIPI PMIC I2C Bus Number 6"), so the I2C bus runs on the mainboard, likely near the PMIC — **not necessarily on the panel connector**. Find it by sweep: 16 LA channels on candidate pads, capture a cold boot, keep the two lines with 400 kHz bursts.
- **No-solder interposition (preferred):** generic **40-pin 0.5 mm FPC breakout board + FFC extension/jumper cable, ~$5–15** (AliExpress/Amazon generic accessory class; order after confirming pitch/pin count at teardown). Sandwich: mainboard → FFC extension → breakout (LA taps) → panel flex. Confidence MEDIUM.
- **No-solder direct probing (fallback):** the U3Pro16 ships **16 hook clips**; Kingst packages grabber clips too. Hook onto the breakout pads or any exposed test points near the connector.
- **Grounding/loading:** LA ground to chassis GND; device runs on battery during capture (floating, safe). LA inputs are ~13 pF / 250 kΩ (U3Pro16 spec) — **fine for LP/I2C taps, destructive for 1 Gbps HS taps**, one more reason HS is out of scope. Expect the screen to glitch or stay dark while HS pairs are tapped with clips; captures are still valid.

## 4. Capture plan

### 4.1 Three captures

| Capture | Window | Mode/settings | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — **GOP boot init** | power-on → ~10 s | 16ch @ 125–500 MS/s, RLE on (LP-11 idles compress hard) | none (arm before power button); GOP repeats on every cold boot, so just repeat captures |
| B — **kernel resume** | suspend + wake + 30 s | stream mode (U3Pro16: 16ch @ 125 MS/s, 16 G ≈ 2 min) | arm, then `rtcwake -m mem -s 20`; or edge-trigger on the 3V3 rail / panel RST |
| C — **PMIC I2C bus 6** | same windows, 60–120 s | 4–8 MS/s stream, I2C decoder + demux by address | same as B |

LA5016 buffer math at 500 MS/s: 2 Gbit / 16ch ≈ 0.25 s raw — insufficient for the ~seconds of boot sequence, so run at **125 MS/s (≈1 s raw, seconds more with RLE)** or use trigger+post-trigger around the RST edge. For capture B prefer the U3Pro16 stream mode, or accept the LA5016 RLE depth.

### 4.2 The HS envelope trick (free with the same capture)

Set the LA threshold ≈ 0.6 V (LA5016/LA2016 thresholds adjustable ±4 V; U3Pro16 0–5 V). Then:
- LP-11 idle (1.2 V on Dp and Dn) reads **high**;
- during an **HS burst** both Dp/Dn collapse to ~200 mV differential swing and read **low**.

So each HS packet appears as a dip on every lane pair. This yields: **HS burst start/stop times, durations, repetition rate, lane participation — everything except content** — from the same capture that decodes LP. Boot-vs-resume HS envelope comparison is directly answerable.

### 4.3 What this loses (documented honestly)

- **Content of HS-format traffic.** If GOP's "direct TCON programming" is HS-mode DCS/MCS writes (block 53 doesn't contain them, so they'd have to come from somewhere — block 55 (258 B, undecoded, raw bytes already dumped in the re_kit) or live HS writes), a cheap LA sees their envelope but not their bytes. Mitigations: decode block 55 statically (zero cost), brute-force DCS replay experiments guided by envelope timing, or panel TCON datasheet hunt (controller still unidentified).
- **HS byte clock measurement** (only envelope timing). Infer lane rate from VBT mode math (~1 Gbps/lane) instead.
- **If it turns out ALL init is HS:** then the instrument's job shrinks to the I2C/rails/envelope plan below, and the content answer moves entirely to static block-55 decoding. That is the §5 "I2C-bus-only" plan, and it is still worth the $238+5 for rails and envelope evidence.

### 4.4 The I2C-bus-only plan (if nothing more is wanted)

LA5016 (or even LA2016/LA1010/fx2lafw clone) on PMIC I2C bus 6 + panel rails: proves which rail toggles GOP performs at boot vs what the kernel resume path performs, at 4 MS/s. Loses all DSI wire content. Cheap, but answers only question (2) of §0.

## 5. Evidence → mechanism story (what changes our understanding)

| Observation in captures | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Boot LP bursts = exactly {`0x11`, 200 ms, `0x29`} + rail toggles, nothing else | GOP's wire traffic = block 53 fully → the unseen TCON programming is **MMIO/PHY-side**, not on the wire → LA exonerates the wire; pivot to block-55 static decode + DPHY register experiments |
| Boot LP bursts contain **extra writes** beyond block 53 | Extra bursts = the GOP's unseen TCON init → decode → replay in kernel = fix candidate |
| Kernel resume re-probe: LP bursts present and identical, panel still black | Kernel sends the "right" DCS but panel-side TCON state diverged → compare HS envelopes + rails; panel datasheet hunt escalates |
| Kernel resume re-probe: **no** LP bursts on the lanes at all | Kernel never transmits (PHY not enabled at wire level) → different bug class than assumed; i915 re-probe audit |
| Rail toggles (I2C bus 6) differ boot vs resume | Wire-level proof of the missing sequencing step; directly actionable in `intel_dsi_vbt`/PPS path |

## 6. The 10 load-bearing facts

1. **Kingst LA5016 — $238** — 16ch @ 500 MS/s, 2 Gbit buffer, ±4 V adjustable threshold, and the **only cheap tool with a built-in "MIPI-DSI LP" decoder** (KingstVIS). `http://www.qdkingst.com/en` (AliExpress official store item 32774297984).
2. **KingstVIS gates the MIPI-DSI LP decoder to LA5016/LA5032 only** — LA1010 ($68) and LA2016 ($138) do NOT get it. `http://www.qdkingst.com/en/plist`.
3. **Kingst LA2016 — $138** — 16ch @ 200 MS/s, 1 Gbit, sigrok-supported (cross-checked sigrok wiki + vendor). Budget fallback; manual/custom decode.
4. **DSLogic U3Pro16 — $299 (was $399)** — 16ch, 500 MS/s buffer @16ch / 125 MS/s stream @16ch, 16 G stream depth (USB3), inputs 250 kΩ//13 pF, 16 hook clips included; no MIPI decoder in DSView. Best for long resume-window streams. `https://www.dreamsourcelab.com/shop/logic-analyzer/dslogic-u3pro16/`.
5. **DSLogic Plus — $149** — 400 MS/s @16ch, 256 M buffer; USB2 streaming limits make it dominated by U3Pro16. `https://www.dreamsourcelab.com/product/dslogic-series/`.
6. **Saleae Logic Pro 16 — 16ch @ 500 MS/s** (`https://www.saleae.com/compare-logic`), ~$1.3k tier, **no DSI decoder** — 500 MS/s cannot even approach 1 Gbps/lane HS (needs 3–5 GS/s). Not recommended for this task.
7. **Verified absence: sigrok/libsigrokdecode (131 decoders) contains NO MIPI DSI decoder** — its "DSI" entry is the Digital Serial Interface *lighting* protocol; DSView inherits the same. Custom PD (~200 lines Python for LP escape) is the open-source route.
8. **The D330 panel's init DCS is LP-mode** (VBT block 53 flags "LP" on the `0x11`/`0x29` DCS writes) → ~20 Mbps escape mode → any 100+ MS/s LA decodes it (LA5016 = 25× oversample). HS video is ~**1 Gbps/lane** (4-lane RGB888 non-burst, 1200×1920@60 portrait, computed from VBT A config) and is **not cheap-decodable** (Wikipedia DSI / mipi.org: HS clock "tens of MHz to over 1 GHz", D-PHY 2.0 to 4.5 Gbps/lane; LP mode is for config/commands).
9. **Panel rails are PMIC-controlled over I2C bus 6** (VBT A: "PPS GPIO Pins: Using PMIC", MIPI PMIC I2C Bus Number 6; GPIO 144=power, 160=reset, 145=backlight) → the I2C bus 6 capture answers the rail-sequencing question even if all DSI init turns out HS-only.
10. **No official schematic/boardview for 82H0** — Lenovo publishes only the free HMM (teardown steps, FRU locations) at `https://support.lenovo.com`; leaked boardviews exist but are unverifiable/unstable → pin identification must be empirical (GND continuity → rails → LP-11 pairs → behavior). Generic **40-pin 0.5 mm FPC breakout + FFC extension ~$5–15** enables no-solder interposition (confirm pitch/pin count at teardown first).

## 7. Recommended purchase

- **Primary:** Kingst **LA5016** — $238. Decode LP DSI out-of-box, covers all three captures (A/B/C).
- **Optional second:** DSLogic **U3Pro16** — $299, only if long uninterrupted stream windows for resume analysis prove painful on the LA5016's buffer. Decide after the first capture session; do not pre-buy.
- **Total if both:** ~$540 + ~$15 FPC hardware. Minimal viable: LA5016 + breakout ≈ **$255**.
